## 1. Executive Overview

Customer churn in retail banking represents more than private revenue loss—it serves as a direct indicator of market competitiveness, deposit flight risk, and consumer dissatisfaction. This investigation analyzed 10,000 retail banking accounts across France, Germany, and Spain to detect systemic churn patterns and quantify financial exposure.

The primary finding is that customer churn is **not uniformly distributed**:
- **Baseline Churn Rate:** **20.37%** (approximately 1 in every 5 accounts leaves).
- **Regional Concentration:** Germany displays a churn rate of **32.44%**, with a Geographic Risk Index of **1.59**, nearly double the churn observed in France (16.15%) and Spain (16.67%).
- **Capital Flight Warning:** Customers with account balances exceeding €119,800 churn at **24.69%**, with churned accounts carrying an average balance of **€91,108.54** compared to **€72,745.30** for retained accounts. High-value depositors represent the highest flight risk.

---

## 2. Key Risk Drivers

### 2.1 Regional Vulnerability (Germany)
German accounts contribute nearly 40% of all churn despite representing only 25% of the total customer base. This disproportionate attrition points toward competitive displacement by digital neo-banks (e.g., N26, DKB) or friction with traditional banking fee structures.

### 2.2 Mature Customer Attrition (Ages 46–60)
The 46–60 age group suffers a **43.29% churn rate**, compared to just 7.55% for customers under 30. This cohort represents primary wage earners with substantial savings, mortgages, and retirement investments who feel underserved by standard retail accounts.

### 2.3 Early Warning Signal: Inactivity Gap
The **Engagement Drop Indicator stands at 12.58 percentage points** (26.85% churn among inactive members versus 14.27% among active members). Dormancy serves as the single strongest operational early warning sign of churn.

### 2.4 Consumer Duty & Gender Variance
Female account holders churn at **25.07%**, compared to **16.46%** for male account holders. This 8.61 percentage point disparity suggests potential gaps in product alignment or customer service accessibility that merit regulatory review under fair consumer treatment mandates.

---

## 3. Regulatory & Policy Recommendations

1. **Mandatory Granular Churn Reporting:**  
   Require domestic systemically important banks (D-SIBs) to report quarterly churn metrics disaggregated by age, geography, balance tier, and activity status to track systemic deposit run risks early.
2. **Deposit Flight Early Warning Monitoring:**  
   Institute thresholds where accounts holding >€100,000 exhibit abnormal outflow velocities, prompting supervisory inquiries into bank liquidity health.
3. **Consumer Switching & Fair Treatment Audits:**  
   Evaluate whether product terms, fee structures, or digital platforms inadvertently disadvantage female or mature depositors, ensuring compliance with European Central Bank (ECB) consumer protection guidelines.
4. **Targeted Financial Advising Standards:**  
   Encourage banks to implement structured financial check-ups for depositors approaching retirement (ages 45–60) to provide suitable wealth preservation solutions and prevent account closures.
